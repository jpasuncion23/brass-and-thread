Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is an "overrun" shirt?

Overruns are extra units made beyond a brand's ordered quantity, or pieces that didn't pass that brand's own quality check for its main retail channel — the print, fabric, and construction are the same, they just never went through that original store. That's the whole reason they're sold at a fraction of the price.

Are these legit, or "tiangge fake"?

Overrun and counterfeit are different things. These are genuine units from the actual production run, sourced through legitimate purchase or consignment with the manufacturer or an authorized surplus distributor — not unauthorized diversion. What you're paying less for is the missing retail markup, not missing quality.

What if the size or color I want sells out?

Since most batches here are one-time acquisitions, a sold-out size or print usually isn't coming back. The stock count on each card is live and updates the moment someone orders — if you see it, it's still there, but we can't promise it'll last.

How do I pay, and when?

Cash on Delivery (COD) or GCash, chosen at checkout. GCash orders are marked Pending until we confirm the payment came through; COD is collected when your order arrives.

How long does delivery take?

We reach out using the contact number you leave at checkout to confirm your order and arrange delivery or pickup timing — this varies by location, so we'll always let you know directly rather than guess here.

Do I need an account to order?

No — checkout works as a guest with just your name, contact number, and payment method. Creating an account is optional and only useful if you want your order history saved for next time.

Can I track my order after checkout?

Yes — log in and check "My Orders" for a live status. You'll also get an email whenever your order's status changes (pending, processing, out for delivery, delivered).

Can I return or exchange an item?

Message us using the contact number on your order as soon as you notice an issue and we'll sort it out case by case — but since batches are often one-time stock, a same-size exchange isn't always possible once a print sells out.
